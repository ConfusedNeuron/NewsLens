## 1. Executive Summary

NewsLens is a personal news intelligence system that transforms raw information from across the web into quantified, actionable impact cards. Unlike traditional news aggregators that tell you *what happened*, NewsLens tells you *what it means in numbers, who benefits, who loses, and how it affects you directly.*

The product is built for people with limited time who need informed perspectives on Finance, Technology, Geopolitics, and Environment — with a dual lens on India and the global stage, with specific focus on the USA and China as key geopolitical and economic counterweights.

---

## 2. Problem Statement

### 2.1 The Problem

Informed people in 2026 face a paradox: there is more news than ever, and less clarity than ever.

- **Volume overload**: Hundreds of relevant articles per day across dozens of sources
- **Surface-level coverage**: Most news tells you the event, not the implication
- **No quantification**: "Markets fell" tells you nothing. "Nifty down 1.8%, bank stocks hardest hit, ₹2.1L Cr market cap erased" tells you something
- **No personalization of impact**: Global macro news has different implications for a salaried engineer in Bengaluru than for a business owner in Surat
- **Fragmented sources**: Finance news, tech developments, and geopolitical shifts are siloed, but they interact constantly

### 2.2 Who Has This Problem

Primary user: Indian professional, 25–45 years old, working in tech, finance, or business. Reads Morning Brew / Finshots / ET occasionally. Wants to be more informed but doesn't have 45 minutes a day for news. Has some financial exposure (mutual funds, a home loan, maybe some stocks). Cares about India first, then the world.

Secondary user: Anyone globally who wants analytical brevity over journalistic volume.

## 6. Feature Requirements

### 6.1 Core Features (v1 — Must Have)

#### F1 — Multi-Source Ingestion Pipeline
- **What**: Automated ingestion from RSS feeds, Reddit, Gmail newsletters, YouTube transcripts, Substack
- **Why**: Single curated stream from high-signal sources eliminates user curation effort
- **Acceptance**: Pipeline runs every 6 hours, ingests from all configured sources without manual intervention

#### F2 — Structured Extraction
- **What**: LLM extracts entities, numbers, event type, and geography from each article into structured JSON
- **Why**: Downstream enrichment and analysis require structured inputs, not free text
- **Acceptance**: > 90% of extractions produce valid JSON; entities correctly identified in manual spot-check

#### F3 — Real-Data Enrichment
- **What**: Stock prices, market cap, macro indicators attached to extracted entities before analysis
- **Why**: LLM analysis grounded in real numbers is more accurate and less likely to hallucinate magnitudes
- **Acceptance**: Enrichment succeeds for > 70% of company/country entities extracted

#### F4 — Quantified Impact Analysis
- **What**: LLM produces winner/loser analysis with estimated magnitude, anchored to enrichment data
- **Why**: This is the core product differentiator — insight, not summary
- **Acceptance**: > 80% of published cards contain at least one quantified winner or loser statement

#### F5 — Swipeable Card UI (Tinder Mode)
- **What**: Centered card with left/right swipe (mouse drag, touch, keyboard arrows)
- **Why**: Frictionless consumption — one insight per card, user controls pace
- **Acceptance**: Works on desktop and mobile browser; swipe registers correctly and advances to next card

#### F6 — Vertical Scroll UI (Reel Mode)
- **What**: Vertical card stack with snap-scroll behavior
- **Why**: Passive consumption mode — scroll through like Instagram Stories
- **Acceptance**: Smooth snap-scroll on mobile; each card fills viewport

#### F7 — Domain and Geography Filters
- **What**: Tab/pill filter for Finance | Tech | Geopolitics | Environment and Global | India | USA | China
- **Why**: Users should be able to narrow the feed to what they care about most
- **Acceptance**: Filter updates card feed in < 500ms; state persists across mode switches

#### F8 — User Profile + Personal Impact Layer
- **What**: 5-question onboarding captures income type, sector exposure, investment profile, city, companies of interest. Analysis prompt uses this profile to generate personalized impact text.
- **Why**: The personal impact layer is the retention hook — generic news apps cannot do this
- **Acceptance**: Personal impact field populated for > 60% of cards when profile exists; text is specific to profile, not generic

#### F9 — Confidence Badge
- **What**: Each card displays a High / Medium / Low confidence badge
- **Why**: Transparency about LLM certainty prevents misplaced trust in uncertain analysis
- **Acceptance**: Badge color-coded (green/amber/grey); low-confidence cards hidden by default with toggle to show

### 6.2 Extended Features (v2 — Should Have)

#### F10 — Nerd Mode (Locked in v1)
- Two-column web layout: source panel left, deep analysis right
- Full winner/loser breakdown with cited numbers
- India / USA / China angle tabs

#### F11 — Weekly Digest
- Email summary of top 10 cards from the week
- Grouped by domain
- Exportable as PDF

#### F12 — Chrome Extension
- Same card UI accessible as browser popup

## 7. User Flows

### 7.1 First Visit Flow
```
Land on site
→ See sample cards (no profile)
→ Onboarding modal: "Personalize your feed" (5 questions)
→ Profile saved to localStorage + API
→ Feed refreshes with personalized impact layer active
→ Choose mode: Tinder or Reel (toggle at top)
→ Begin consuming cards
```

### 7.2 Returning User Flow
```
Land on site
→ Profile loaded from localStorage
→ Feed loads latest cards filtered by last-used domain/geo settings
→ Continue swiping
```

### 7.3 Pipeline Flow (Background)
```
APScheduler triggers every 6 hours
→ All ingest agents run in parallel
→ Clean agent runs on new raw items
→ Extract agent runs on clean items (LLM #1)
→ Classify agent tags and scores items
→ Enrich agent fetches market/macro data
→ Analyze agent generates impact analysis (LLM #2)
→ Format agent creates cards
→ Quality gate approves/discards/holds cards
→ Approved cards pushed to live feed
```

## 8. Domain Analysis SOPs

### 8.1 Finance
- **Lens**: What moves, and in which direction
- **Winners**: Sectors, companies, instrument holders who benefit from the event
- **Losers**: Counterparts who face headwinds
- **Magnitude**: Anchored to % change, rupee/dollar value, rate basis points
- **Time horizon**: Immediate (today), Short (1–3 months), Long (1+ year)
- **India angle**: RBI policy implications, FII flows, Nifty/Sensex sector impact
- **USA angle**: Fed policy, S&P sector impact, dollar strength
- **China angle**: PBOC policy, export competitiveness, trade flow implications

### 8.2 Technology
- **Lens**: What capability shifts, what market share changes
- **Winners**: Companies gaining capability, developers with new tools, consumers with better products
- **Losers**: Incumbents being disrupted, workers displaced, competing platforms losing relevance
- **Magnitude**: User/developer numbers, revenue estimates, market share %
- **India angle**: IT sector impact, startup ecosystem effect, talent demand signal
- **USA angle**: Big tech power shift, regulation risk, VC sentiment
- **China angle**: Tech sovereignty angle, chip access, AI competition

### 8.3 Geopolitics
- **Lens**: What changes in power, policy, or alliances
- **Winners**: Nations, blocs, political parties, demographics that gain influence or stability
- **Losers**: Nations or groups facing isolation, economic pressure, or instability
- **Magnitude**: Proxy metrics — trade volume affected, population impacted, treaty scope
- **India angle**: Strategic positioning, border security, trade implications, diaspora effect
- **USA angle**: Alliance strength, global influence, domestic political implications
- **China angle**: Belt & Road impact, regional influence, Taiwan strait dynamics

### 8.4 Environment
- **Lens**: What measurable change or commitment is being made
- **Winners**: Affected regions gaining protection, clean energy sector, future generations
- **Losers**: Fossil fuel sector, high-emission industries, regions with stranded assets
- **Magnitude**: Emissions numbers, temperature targets, fund sizes, affected area (sq km, population)
- **India angle**: Monsoon/agricultural impact, energy transition pace, green finance flows
- **USA angle**: IRA implementation, clean energy investment, political headwinds
- **China angle**: Solar/EV supply chain, carbon market, coal dependency

## 10. Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| LLM hallucination of numbers | Medium | High | Enrich before analyze; numbers must be grounded in enrichment data or marked as "estimated" |
| Source goes down / changes format | High | Low | Per-source error handling; pipeline continues without failed source |
| Gmail OAuth complexity | Medium | Medium | Optional feature; pipeline works without it |
| Reddit API rate limits | Low | Low | Respect 60 req/min limit; cache aggressively |
| Low card quality at launch | Medium | High | Quality gate discards weak cards; better to show fewer good cards |
| Token costs exceed budget | Low | Medium | 2 LLM calls per article max; dedup reduces volume significantly |

## 12. Open Questions

1. Should cards generated from cross-domain events be shown once or shown in each relevant domain tab?
2. What is the right pipeline interval? 6 hours may be too slow for breaking news. Consider a "breaking" tier at 1-hour intervals for top sources only.
3. How should the personal impact layer handle users who haven't completed onboarding? Generic impact or no impact field?
4. Should YouTube transcript chunks be treated as full articles or merged back before analysis?
5. What's the right way to handle conflicting signals across sources on the same event (e.g. Reuters says positive, Reddit says negative)?
